\section{Differential KV Compression}
\label{sec:compression}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering

A block of contiguous KV vectors is highly redundant---neighboring tokens share most of their
representation---but it is not \emph{uniformly} redundant: a handful of tokens carry distinctive,
low-energy detail (a rare entity, a passcode) that a pure low-rank model discards. \diffkv{}'s
block representation reflects this directly: an anchor plus a low-rank differential captures the
shared structure cheaply, and a small set of \emph{exact residual tokens} preserves the
outliers the low-rank term cannot. Both parts are computed once per block during prefill.

\subsection{Notation}
Within one layer, a block of $\bs{=}256$ contiguous tokens has keys
$K\in\R^{\Hkv\times \bs\times \dhead}$ and values $V$ of the same shape
($\Hkv{=}2$, $\dhead{=}128$). We compress blocks independently and per layer.

\subsection{Anchor + differential representation}
The first token of the block is the \emph{anchor}; per head $h$ we keep its key and value
exactly:
\begin{equation}
\anchork^{(h)} = K^{(h)}_{0}, \qquad \anchorv^{(h)} = V^{(h)}_{0}.
\end{equation}
For the remaining $S{=}\bs-1$ tokens we form deltas against the anchor,
\begin{equation}
\Delta K^{(h)}_{i} = K^{(h)}_{i} - \anchork^{(h)}, \quad
\Delta V^{(h)}_{i} = V^{(h)}_{i} - \anchorv^{(h)}, \quad i=1{\dots}S,
\end{equation}
and stack them across heads into a single matrix per modality. Concatenating keys and values
gives the joint delta matrix
\begin{equation}
X = \big[\,\Delta K \;\big|\; \Delta V\,\big]\in\R^{S\times 2\Hkv\dhead},
\label{eq:X}
\end{equation}
i.e. $X\in\R^{255\times 512}$ here. Joint factorization is deliberate: it forces keys and
values of the same token to share one low-rank coefficient vector, halving the per-token
coefficient storage relative to factorizing them separately.

\subsection{Normalized randomized truncated SVD}
Tokens within a block differ widely in delta magnitude, and an unnormalized SVD spends its
budget on the largest-norm rows. We therefore $\ell_2$-normalize each row before
factorization, storing the norms separately:
\begin{equation}
n_i = \lVert X_i\rVert_2,\qquad \hat X_i = X_i / \max(n_i,\,\epsilon).
\end{equation}
$\hat X$ is then factorized by a randomized truncated SVD~\cite{halko2011randomized} at target
rank $\rank{=}16$: a global scale $s=\max|\hat X|$ is divided out, an oversampled Gaussian
range finder ($\rank{+}5$ columns) with two power iterations and a QR step yields an
orthonormal basis $Q$, and a small projected SVD of $Q^{\!\top}\hat X$ gives
$\hat X \approx U_k\Sigma_k V_k^{\!\top}$. An \emph{adaptive} rank keeps the fewest components
explaining $99.9\%$ of the spectral energy, clamped to $[4,\rank]$. The Gaussian is drawn from
a \emph{fixed seed}, so the factorization---and therefore decode output---is reproducible to the
bit. The stored factors are
\begin{equation}
U = (U_k\Sigma_k)\odot n \in\R^{S\times \rank},\qquad
V^{\!\top} = V_k^{\!\top}\in\R^{\rank\times 2\Hkv\dhead},
\end{equation}
where the row-norms $n$ are re-applied to $U$ so that reconstruction recovers the true delta
scale. $V^{\!\top}$ is split back into key and value bases $V_K,V_V\in\R^{\Hkv\times\rank\times\dhead}$.
The implicit reconstruction of any token---never materialized in practice---is
\begin{equation}
\hat K^{(h)}_i \;\approx\; \anchork^{(h)} + s\,\big(U_i\, V_K^{(h)}\big),
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
and analogously for values. We run the SVD in NumPy: on unified memory the array is the same
bytes the GPU uses, so the only cost is compute, and a $255{\times}512$ factorization is a few
milliseconds---amortized over the $256$ tokens it represents.

\subsection{Exact residual tokens: restoring the outliers SVD discards}
\label{sec:residuals}
A rank-$16$ factorization of a $255{\times}512$ block captures the dominant directions but
truncates low-energy ones, and a single distinctive token (e.g.\ a buried passcode) is precisely
a low-energy outlier: its delta is nearly orthogonal to the block's principal subspace, so its
reconstruction error is large while its contribution to the spectrum is small. We therefore
measure the per-token reconstruction error against the stored factors,
\begin{equation}
e_i = \big\lVert X_i - s\,(U_i V^{\!\top})\big\rVert_2, \qquad i=1{\dots}S,
\end{equation}
and keep the $\maxres{=}64$ highest-error tokens' \emph{exact} fp16 key/value pairs as
\emph{residuals} $R_K, R_V\in\R^{\maxres\times\Hkv\times\dhead}$, stored highest-error-first.
Top-by-error selection is deliberate: an earlier median/MAD outlier threshold regressed, because
on-topic prose filler has a broad error spread that lifts the threshold above the needle's tokens
and drops them. A buried needle can also span several mid-rank residuals, so the budget must be
generous---at $\maxres{=}32$ the later tokens of a multi-token passcode were truncated and their
values corrupted; $\maxres{=}64$ retains all of a content-dense block's distinctive tokens. At
decode the residuals are attended \emph{exactly} (\S\ref{sec:decode}), so they recover verbatim
detail that the low-rank term alone cannot---this is the mechanism behind \diffkv{}'s exact
needle recall.

We additionally store the element-wise key min and max over the block,
$k^{\min}, k^{\max}\in\R^{\Hkv\times\dhead}$; these support an optional cheap block router
(\S\ref{sec:decode}) and cost two anchor-sized tensors.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textsc{CompressBlock} (one block, one layer)}
\label{alg:compress}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require block keys $K$, values $V$ $\in\R^{\Hkv\times \bs\times \dhead}$; rank $\rank$; residual budget $\maxres$
\State $\anchork \gets K_{:,0,:};\ \anchorv \gets V_{:,0,:}$
\State $\Delta K \gets K_{:,1:,:}-\anchork;\quad \Delta V \gets V_{:,1:,:}-\anchorv$
\State $X \gets \textsc{StackHeads}([\Delta K \mid \Delta V])$ \Comment{$\R^{S\times 2\Hkv\dhead}$}
\State $n_i \gets \lVert X_i\rVert_2;\quad \hat X \gets X / n$ \Comment{row normalize}
\State $U_k,\Sigma_k,V_k,s,k \gets \textsc{RandTruncSVD}(\hat X,\rank)$ \Comment{fixed seed; adaptive $k\le\rank$}
\State $U \gets (U_k\Sigma_k)\odot n;\quad [V_K\mid V_V]\gets V_k^{\!\top}$
\State $e_i \gets \lVert X_i - s\,(U_i V_k^{\!\top})\rVert_2$ \Comment{per-token recon.\ error}
\State $\mathcal{T}\gets$ indices of the $\maxres$ largest $e_i$;\ \ $R_K,R_V \gets K_{:,\mathcal{T},:}, V_{:,\mathcal{T},:}$
\State $k^{\min},k^{\max}\gets \min_i K_{:,i,:},\ \max_i K_{:,i,:}$
\State \Return $(U,V_K,V_V,\anchork,\anchorv,s,\,R_K,R_V,\,k^{\min},k^{\max})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Storage budget and complexity}
Table~\ref{tab:budget} gives the exact per-block byte budget. The compressed block stores the
shared coefficients $U$ ($S\rank$), two bases $V_K,V_V$ ($2\Hkv\rank\dhead$), two anchors and the
key min/max ($4\Hkv\dhead$), two scalars, \emph{and} the $\maxres$ exact residual KV pairs
($\maxres\cdot 2\Hkv\dhead$). The residuals dominate the block---at $\maxres{=}64$ they are
$65{,}536$ of the $92{,}136$ bytes---so they must be counted: the honest reduction at equal
(fp16) precision is $262{,}144 / 92{,}136 = \mathbf{2.85\times}$ per block, not the
$10\times$ the low-rank term alone would suggest. This is the price of exact recall, and
\S\ref{sec:eval} quantifies the recall/footprint trade-off as $\maxres$ varies.

\noindent
Asymptotically, a compressed block costs $O(\bs\rank + \Hkv\rank\dhead + \maxres\Hkv\dhead)$
versus the dense $O(\bs\Hkv\dhead)$. With the residual budget a fixed fraction
$\maxres/\bs = 64/256$ of the block kept exact, the marginal per-token cost is a constant
fraction of the dense cost plus an $O(\rank)$ low-rank term---bounded below dense but, unlike a
pure low-rank scheme, not asymptotically vanishing. The factorization itself is
$O(S\cdot 2\Hkv\dhead\cdot \rank)$ per block and fires once per $\bs$ tokens, so its amortized
per-token compute is $O(\Hkv\dhead\rank)$---the prefill cost we quantify in \S\ref{sec:eval}.
