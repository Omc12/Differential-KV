\section{Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{What the compression actually buys}
The cleanest statement of \diffkv{}'s benefit is at the level of KV state, not allocator peak.
The compressed store is a \emph{pre-allocated, fixed} pool: its footprint is a constant of the
configuration ($\approx\!0.68$\,GB across $28$ layers) regardless of context, whereas the dense
full-KV cache grows linearly and crosses the store's size early and keeps going
(Fig.~\ref{fig:g1}, Table~\ref{tab:main}). The reduction is $\approx\!2.85\times$ per block at
equal precision---a real but modest factor, because a quarter of every block is kept exact as
residuals. Compression alone, then, is not the whole story; the system-level win is that a
\emph{bounded} store plus the cache-drop at the prefill$\to$decode boundary lets the runtime
address contexts the dense cache cannot fit on the same device (\S\ref{sec:eval}, E2). The
$\rank$-vs-$\Hkv\dhead$ gap of \S\ref{sec:compression} sets the low-rank part of that bound; the
residual budget sets the rest.

\subsection{Why the allocator peak tells a different story}
The MLX allocator peak is nearly identical for the compressed runtime, the exact-decode ablation,
and the dense baseline at matched context, because the peak is set during \emph{prefill}---a full
attention over the growing native cache that all three perform---and is dominated by weights and
transient activations rather than by the retained KV state (Fig.~\ref{fig:g2}). Compression reduces
the \emph{steady-state} decode footprint, not the prefill peak. We make this explicit rather than
reporting only a process-level memory number that would credit compression with a peak reduction it
does not deliver. The practical implication is that on a memory-constrained device the binding
event is whichever is larger of (a) the prefill peak and (b) the decode-time resident set;
\diffkv{} lowers (b) and bounds it, but leaves (a) to prefill-side techniques (chunking, activation
release) the runtime already applies. The reach result of E2 is precisely the regime where (b),
accumulated over a long generation, is what would otherwise exhaust memory.

\subsection{The throughput cost}
The compressed decode is several times slower than dense decode and roughly flat across context
(Fig.~\ref{fig:g3}). The flatness follows from the compute profile of \S\ref{sec:decode}: with a
fixed top-$K$, the low-rank aggregation and the exact-residual attention are context-independent,
and only the router is linear in context with a small $R\dhead$ constant. The absolute gap is an
implementation property, not an algorithmic necessity: the kernel expresses many small tensor
contractions inside one compiled graph, and on this hardware that does not reach the throughput of
MLX's native fused attention over a contiguous cache. The exact-decode ablation---identical store,
attention over the retained native cache---recovers dense-like throughput, which localizes the gap
to the compressed-kernel dispatch rather than to anything the compression or routing precludes. A
hand-written fused decode (a custom Metal op, or CUDA/Triton on NVIDIA) is the natural way to close
it, and the adaptive policy avoids paying it at all until context makes memory the binding cost.

\subsection{Fidelity: restored by residuals, at a memory price}
\label{sec:fidelity}
A pure low-rank block cannot reproduce a buried single-token detail once it leaves the recency
window: the needle's delta is a low-energy outlier that rank-$16$ truncation discards. \diffkv{}
resolves this not by raising the rank but by keeping the block's highest-reconstruction-error
tokens \emph{exactly} (\S\ref{sec:residuals}) and attending them at full precision
(\S\ref{sec:decode}). With the default budget the compressed decode recovers the needle at every
tested context (\S\ref{sec:eval}, E5)---the qualitative correctness the residual-free version
lacked. The cost is memory: E6 (Fig.~\ref{fig:g6}, Table~\ref{tab:residual}) shows recall turning
on as the budget grows while the store grows with it. The residual-key router
(Eq.~\ref{eq:router}) is the second half of the fix---a low-rank or min/max \emph{summary} score
averages the outlier away and can drop the needle's block from the top-$K$ at large block counts,
whereas scoring the exact residual keys keeps it. Both mechanisms are content- and
model-agnostic: they act on whichever tokens a block finds distinctive, with no tuning to the
needle.

\begin{observation}
\diffkv{} trades \emph{decode throughput and a modest amount of memory} (the residuals) for
\emph{bounded, context-independent KV state with exact retrieval}. The trade is favorable exactly
when the KV cache is the binding constraint---long context on a memory-limited device---and the
throughput cost is acceptable or removed by a fused kernel; it is unfavorable when the device has
memory to spare, where the adaptive policy simply decodes exactly.
\end{observation}
