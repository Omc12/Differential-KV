\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limits}

We list the limitations plainly; several are direct consequences of the design choices above.

\paragraph{Decode throughput.} The headline cost. \diffkv{} decode is slower than a dense cache
that fits (\S\ref{sec:eval}, E4), and the E6 ablation localizes the gap to per-token
reconstruction-and-merge overhead. The clearest remedy is a single fused decode kernel that does
the routing, low-rank scoring, residual attention, and merge in one launch, rather than a sequence
of MLX ops.

\paragraph{CUDA / Triton path (reserved, not evaluated).} A CUDA/Triton decode kernel exists in
the repository, but the evaluation host has no NVIDIA GPU, so we report \emph{no} CUDA numbers and
make \emph{no} CUDA claims. The decode design ports cleanly---reconstruct routed blocks into a
buffer (a matmul), attend with a flash kernel, cache across a re-route interval---so we expect the
gap in E4 to narrow on a GPU-native path, but that is a hypothesis to be measured, not a result.
The CUDA kernel is additionally equipped with \path{@triton.autotune}~\cite{tillet2019triton}
over tiling configurations (see \S\ref{par:ktransformers}), and the runtime's tiered CPU--GPU
block offloading (\S\ref{par:ktransformers}) is active on CUDA with asynchronous H2D via dedicated
\path{cudaStream}s. Both are CPU-verified and ready for hardware validation.
The results tables keep a column convention so CUDA data can be inserted without restructuring the
paper.

\paragraph{Single model, single host.} Every number here is Qwen2.5-1.5B (int4) on one Apple~M3.
The residual budget and router settings are tuned for this model; larger models, different
architectures, and non-Apple hardware are unvalidated. Two normalizations (the $V$-to-$K$ RMS
rescale, the seeded rank) are model-sensitive and should be re-checked per model.

\paragraph{Evaluation breadth.} Correctness here is exact single-needle recall plus fluent
long-form generation. This is a strong, unambiguous signal for the fidelity claim, but it is not a
substitute for standard long-context suites (RULER, LongBench), multi-needle and multi-hop recall,
or comparisons against other cache-compression baselines (quantization, eviction, other low-rank
methods). Diffuse multi-fact synthesis, where the top-\topk{} router must cover several regions at
once, is a known weaker case that the single-needle metric does not probe; an optional log-sum-exp
bias mitigates it but is not a full solution. Broadening the evaluation is the most important next
step for external credibility.

\paragraph{Memory-bound long context on an 8\,GB host.} At $64$k, both \diffkv{} and the optimized
dense are pushed to the machine's limit: their prefills complete but are heavily memory-bound (so the
$64$k prefill \emph{times} are reach indicators, not clean measurements), while the standard PyTorch
dense has already OOM'd at $\ptOOMK$k. A machine with more memory headroom would give cleaner
absolute long-context timings and would move both the naive-dense OOM boundary and the
optimized-dense limit further out; the robust claims are the sub-quadratic prefill scaling, the
$\pfSpeedupSixtyFourK\times$ prefill lead at $\reachK$k, and the bounded KV state, not the specific
$64$k prefill seconds.

\paragraph{Bounded pool.} The fixed $256$-block pool caps compressed capacity at $65{,}536$ tokens.
Beyond that, the oldest blocks must be dropped or the pool enlarged (trading memory). A capacity
policy for contexts past the pool is future work.
